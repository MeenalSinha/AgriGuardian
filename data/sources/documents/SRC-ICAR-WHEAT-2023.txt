SECTION 1: PHYSIOLOGICAL GROWTH STAGES & CRITICAL IRRIGATION (Pages 12-16)
Wheat (Triticum aestivum) has six critical growth stages for moisture management:
1. Crown Root Initiation (CRI): 20 to 25 Days After Sowing (DAS)
2. Tillering stage: 40 to 45 DAS
3. Late Jointing: 60 to 65 DAS
4. Flowering stage: 80 to 85 DAS
5. Milking stage: 100 to 105 DAS
6. Dough stage: 115 to 120 DAS

CRI Stage Vulnerability (Page 14):
"If only one irrigation is available, it must be applied at Crown Root Initiation (21-25 DAS) to secure root anchoring and tiller primordia."
Moisture stress during this 5-day window prevents seminal root transitioning to coronal root architecture, reducing productive tillers per plant by up to 35% and inflicting an irreversible 30-40% grain yield penalty.

SECTION 2: METEOROLOGICAL STRESS & CLIMATE RESILIENCE (Pages 18-22)
1. Terminal Heat Stress:
In Northern and Central India, sudden temperature spikes during the grain filling and milking stages (March-April) exceeding 30°C to 32°C accelerate crop maturity, curtail assimilate translocation, reduce thousand-grain weight, and cause shrivelled grains. Light sprinkler or furrow irrigation during sudden warm spells provides micro-climatic evaporative cooling.

2. Lodging Risk & Wind Hazards:
Tall varieties and well-fertilized lush canopies are susceptible to root or stem lodging when irrigation coincides with strong convective winds (>35-40 km/h) during heading and grain development stages. Never irrigate wheat fields when high winds or gusty rainstorms are forecast within 24-48 hours.

SECTION 3: STRIPE RUST (YELLOW RUST) EPIDEMIOLOGY (Pages 25-29)
Pathogen: Puccinia striiformis f. sp. tritici
Symptomatology: Linear, bright yellow powdery pustules arranged in parallel stripes along leaf blades.
Epidemiological Triggers (Page 27):
"Continuous dew formation with daytime temperatures below 15°C accelerates yellow rust sporulation on susceptible varieties."
Optimal conditions are day temperatures between 10°C and 16°C combined with high atmospheric humidity (>80%) and persistent early morning fog or intermittent drizzle in the sub-mountainous tarai and plains tracts of Punjab, Haryana, and Western UP from late December through mid-February.
